## 4. Cash tax versus underlying tax (what can and cannot be concluded)

- Cash tax paid in 2026 (127.1bn) is **1.88 times** the underlying charge (67.5bn) and **7.6 times** the reported charge (16.8bn).
- Cash tax paid is a cash-flow measure. It includes payments on prior-year liabilities, advance tax, and possibly final taxes. The reported charge is a P&L measure after the 50.8bn credit.
- The SOFP shows "Income tax – advance" of Rs 222,440,271 (con SOFP, PDF p56; printed page not verified) as an asset. Cash paid can therefore be large while the P&L charge is small, if advance tax is being recovered against future liabilities or if the reversal of the provision reduced the liability.
- **Not concluded:** a full reconciliation from tax expense to cash tax. It needs the current and deferred tax note, the movement in tax payable and advance tax, and final-tax detail. None of these is reconciled in this file; no reconciling item has been forced.

## 5. Is the 50.8bn credit recurring?

- Per note 35.1, the credit is an "effect of prior year charge" reversal following a favourable outcome of a demand; the note says the demand "does not hold any merit under the applicable tax laws", and no provision was made for the reversed amount. The reversal relates to prior years.
- Recorded as **non-recurring in the reported 2026 charge**. Components that may recur: the underlying 26.06% rate is itself not forecast-verified; the 2025 ETR was 39.2% and the 2026 underlying 26.1% differs by 13pp, which means the historical rate is not a stable forecast rate.
- **Not verified:** the amount, its basis and whether any disputed demand remains open (note 35.1 text, PDF p38, read in full would be required for the remaining demand).
