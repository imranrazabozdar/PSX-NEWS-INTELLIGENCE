# HUBC — The Hub Power Company Ltd · Research Brief (Part 1: Financials & Drivers)

**Prepared from:** audited consolidated accounts FY2024–FY2026 (read from filed statements) + Investify market data.
**Basis:** consolidated, PKR. Shares ≈ 1,297m. Price ref ≈ Rs 207 (12 Nov 2026).
**Nature of this document:** decision-support research. Not a buy/sell recommendation.

---

## 1. The one thing to understand first

**HUBC is not a power plant — it is a power-investment holding company.** The market often values it on the old Hub base plant; that misses where the money is:

- **Share of profit from associates & JVs was Rs 45.3bn in FY26 — about 65% of pre-tax profit**, and *larger than the entire profit from operations of the group's own plants* (Rs 34.1bn).
- **Long-term investments of Rs 207bn = ~49% of total assets** (Rs 423bn), versus own property, plant & equipment of Rs 119bn (28%).

So HUBC's earnings and value are overwhelmingly its stakes in **CPHGC (coal), China Power Hub, Narowal, Thar/ThalNova and related JVs** — equity-accounted. Analyse the portfolio, not the Hub plant.

## 2. Three-year trend: declining core, resilient bottom line

| Rs bn (consolidated) | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 130.5 | 83.4 | 71.1 |
| Gross profit | 68.3 | 39.8 | 29.7 |
| Profit from operations | 67.4 | 38.0 | 34.1 |
| Finance costs | (26.7) | (15.2) | (9.1) |
| Share of associates/JV | 49.4 | 41.3 | 45.3 |
| Net profit | 75.3 | 51.8 | 56.3 |
| EPS (Rs) | 53.98 | 35.56 | 38.26 |

- **Core is shrinking fast:** revenue −45% in two years, gross profit −57%. The old Hub base plant is winding down (PPA maturity, lower dispatch).
- **Yet EPS rose +7.6% in FY26** (35.56 → 38.26) *despite* revenue −15%. Two engines offset the declining core: **finance costs more than halved** (26.7 → 9.1bn, from deleveraging + falling rates) and **associate income grew** (+10% in FY26). This is the holding-company model carrying the group as the base plant fades.

## 3. The cash story: the thesis turned in FY26

For years the bear case was that HUBC's profits were *paper* — equity-accounted associate earnings that couldn't be pulled out as cash because of circular debt. FY26 is the inflection:

- **Cash dividends received from associates: Rs 0.2bn (FY25) → Rs 43.9bn (FY26).** The associates finally upstreamed cash.
- That funded a **dividend hike from Rs 15 to Rs 20/share** (dividends paid to owners Rs 17.5bn → Rs 32.4bn), shifted toward interim payouts (Rs 15 interim in FY26).

**The important nuance (earnings quality):** core operating cash *collapsed* — net cash from operations Rs 78.8bn (FY25) → Rs 11.4bn (FY26) — because FY25 contained a one-off Rs 52.4bn working-capital release (parent circular-debt recovery) that did not repeat. So the group now **leans on associate dividends, not its own operations, to fund the payout.** The Rs 20 dividend is backed by associate cash upstreaming continuing — that is the key dependency.

## 4. Balance sheet: strong, deleveraging

- Total equity Rs 258bn; equity attributable to owners Rs 235bn (book value ≈ Rs 181/sh group, ~Rs 207/sh incl. reserves per Investify).
- Long-term loans down Rs 71.8bn → 62.9bn; **finance-cost tailwind should continue into FY27** as rates fall and debt amortises.
- Unappropriated profit Rs 213bn — ample distributable reserve.
- **Watch item:** trade debts still rising (Rs 29.0bn → 32.4bn) — circular debt not fully resolved at the parent.

## 5. Valuation (from market data — indicative)

- P/E ≈ **5.4x** (EPS 38.26, price ~207) · Dividend yield ≈ **9.7%** (Rs 20) · P/B ≈ **1.0x**.
- Cheap on the surface, with EPS growing and a de-risking cash story. The low multiple prices in: a declining/end-of-life core, historically paper earnings, circular-debt overhang, Pakistan macro/political risk, and **IPP tariff-renegotiation policy risk**.
- Bull case: long-life associate portfolio (Thar coal is local-fuel), FY26 proved cash upstreaming works, deleveraging + rate cuts lift EPS, ~10% yield while you wait, and the BYD EV venture as free optionality.

## 6. Driver model — what the stock actually depends on

1. **Associate cash upstreaming** (CPHGC, Thar) — *the* key variable now. FY26 proved it; continuation is the thesis.
2. **Circular debt / government payment behaviour** — drives both parent receivables and associate cash.
3. **Interest rates** — Rs 63bn debt + discount rate on a high-yield equity; falling rates = EPS tailwind + potential re-rating.
4. **IPP policy / tariff renegotiation** — government pressure on IPP returns is the main policy risk. [TO RESEARCH + CITE]
5. **PKR/USD** — dollar-indexation in PPAs; relevant to associate economics. [TO RESEARCH + CITE]
6. **Thar coal / fuel economics** for CPHGC — local fuel insulates from imported-coal prices. [TO RESEARCH + CITE]
7. **EV venture (BYD via Hub Power Holdings)** — early-stage optionality; a re-rating catalyst if it scales, unproven today.
