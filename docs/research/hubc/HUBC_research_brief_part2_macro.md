# HUBC — Research Brief (Part 2: Macro & Policy) · sourced

All external facts below are cited. Neutral presentation of policy; no political view.

## 1. The IPP policy context — and why HUBC's revenue collapsed

The revenue drop (Rs 130bn → 71bn over FY24–FY26) is now explained by policy, not operational failure:
- HUBC's original **1,292MW furnace-oil Hub base plant had its power-purchase agreement terminated early, effective 1 October 2024** — one of six IPPs terminated in a government drive to cut capacity payments (projected ~Rs411bn savings). *(Dawn, Dec 2024; csspmsnotes, 2025)*
- So FY25 lost ~9 months of base-plant revenue and FY26 lost it entirely. The declining core is a *known, policy-driven wind-down of an end-of-life plant*, not a surprise.
- Capacity payments had ballooned to ~Rs2.1tr in FY24; the government renegotiated 14 further IPPs in Jan 2025 (~Rs1.4tr lifetime savings) and aimed to finalise all remaining IPP agreements by June 2026. *(csspmsnotes; The News, 2026)*

**The crucial nuance for HUBC's value:** most of HUBC's earnings come from its **associates/JVs — above all CPHGC, a 1.32GW CPEC coal plant (2019)** — plus Thar Energy, ThalNova, Narowal, Laraib. Commentary notes the **CPEC/G2G plants (~75% of capacity payments) are the hardest for the government to renegotiate** (sovereign agreements with China). *(Dawn, 2025)* So HUBC's core value engine (CPHGC) sits in the bucket most protected from unilateral tariff cuts — a meaningful defensive feature, while the exposed, low-value base plant is already gone.

## 2. Circular debt — the persistent overhang

- Power-sector circular debt was ~**Rs1.84tr (Feb 2026), reaching Rs1.835tr by June 2026, exceeding the IMF ceiling (Rs1.614tr) by ~Rs221bn.** *(propakistani, profit.pk, July 2026)*
- A $36bn refinancing plan was shelved in April 2026; the government targets zero *net* addition and elimination by FY31. *(profit.pk, Apr 2026; nukta)*
- Implication: the overhang that historically trapped HUBC's associate cash is easing but not resolved — consistent with HUBC's FY26 Rs 43.9bn associate-dividend breakthrough yet still-rising parent trade debts (Rs 29.0bn → 32.4bn).

## 3. Interest rates — CORRECTION to Part 1: the rate tailwind has reversed

Part 1 framed "falling rates" as a forward tailwind. The sourced timeline shows rates **fell into FY26 but have since turned up:**
- Cut to **10.5% (Dec 2025)**, held Jan 2026, then **raised to 11.5% (Apr 2026)**, held through Sep 2026, then **raised to 12.0% effective 27 Oct 2026** — the first rise since April, on a worsening inflation outlook and geopolitics. *(ARY News, Oct 2026; SBP; Tribune)*
- So HUBC's FY26 (year to June 2026) benefited from a lower average rate — finance costs more than halved — but **FY27 faces rising rates.** The *deleveraging* tailwind continues (debt amortising, loans Rs 72bn → 63bn), but the *rate* tailwind has paused/reversed. Net FY27 finance-cost benefit is likely more muted than FY26's.

## 4. EV / auto policy — a genuine tailwind for the optionality

- The **Autos & Auto Parts Manufacturing Policy 2026–2031** (announced 7 Nov 2026; PM-approved, pending Cabinet + IMF) and the underlying **New Energy Vehicle Policy 2025–2030 target 30% EV share of new vehicle sales by 2030.** *(Dawn, ArabNews, Nov 2026)*
- Incentives: concessional 1% sales tax on pure EVs (pending IMF), EVs ≤$50,000 exempt from CVT/WHT/FED, SBP EV financing raised Rs3m→Rs10m (tenure 3→5yr), charging equipment at 1% GST with government viability-gap funding, and a 5% concessional duty on BEVs ≤$15,000 *conditional on local assembly* (commercial production by 30 Sep 2028). *(Dawn, brecorder, Nov 2026)*
- Directly relevant: **BYD is commissioning a Gharo, Sindh assembly plant by end-2026 (25,000 vehicles/yr, scaling to 50,000), and Hub Power Holdings is its partner** for EVs and charging infrastructure. *(ArabNews, Nov 2026)*
- Read: the policy specifically rewards *local-assembly* EV players — exactly HUBC/BYD's model — making the EV optionality real and policy-supported, though early-stage (plant just commissioning, not yet material in the numbers). Treat as a call option, not base-case earnings.

## 6. Synthesis

HUBC is a **deleveraging holding company whose value is a portfolio of relatively-protected CPEC/Thar power stakes, now finally converting paper earnings to cash, paying a ~10% yield, with a free EV option on top** — against a backdrop of a declining legacy plant (already de-rated), an easing-but-unresolved circular-debt system, and a rate cycle that has turned less favourable. The ~5.4x P/E prices the risks heavily; the FY26 cash breakthrough and CPEC protection are the under-appreciated positives. Key monitorable: whether associate cash upstreaming (the dividend's real backing) continues.
