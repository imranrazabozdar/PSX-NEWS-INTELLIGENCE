# HUBC — Research Brief (Part 3: Analyst & Management views)

Sources: SCS/Standard Capital 9MFY26 Result Review (22 Apr 2026); HUBC Corporate Briefing takeaways (2 Sep 2026). These triangulate the statement analysis in Parts 1–2.

## Corroboration (independent sources agree with the raw numbers)
- FY26 net profit (owners) **PKR 49.6bn, +8% YoY, EPS 38.26**; growth from **+10% associate income and −40% finance costs** — matches my read exactly.
- Deleveraging: total debt **PKR 63.4bn from 74.7bn**. Dividend **Rs20/sh (9.5% yield)**. At ~Rs208: **P/E 5.4x, P/B 1.05x**. All confirmed.
- SCS frames the thesis as an "**earnings shift** from core power to JVs after base-plant capacity payments ended" — the same holding-company reality I identified.

## New insight 1 — the associate income is diversified, and dispatch is low
Management plant load factors FY26: **TEL (Thar Energy) 69%, TNTPL (ThalNova) 69%, Laraib 48%, CPHGC 11%, Narowal 9%.**
- The **local Thar-coal plants (TEL/TNTPL) are the workhorses** (69% load) and **began paying dividends after project completion** — a key reason FY26 associate *cash* finally flowed.
- **CPHGC (1,320MW imported coal) ran at only 11%** — it earns largely **capacity payments** (paid for availability, dispatched last because imported coal is expensive in the merit order). So CPHGC is a capacity-payment asset, not a high-dispatch generator — important nuance vs. treating it as "the" engine.

## New insight 2 — hidden assets / optionality not in the ratios
- **~1,100 acres of base-plant land** freed by the PPA termination, under feasibility for an **aluminium smelter or oil-storage facility**. The old plant's residual value is the *land*, not generation.
- **Power distribution ambition is broad:** exploring privatisation of **IESCO, FESCO and GEPCO** — a genuine push from generation into distribution.
- **E&P + mining + lithium:** Prime Energy (ENI-linked Sindh wells), ARK Metals (Balochistan drilling), stated lithium potential pending mining policy.
These are real call options the 5.4x P/E gives little credit for.

## New insight 3 — EV is management's #1 near-term growth driver (more developed than headlines)
- Target **30% NEV market share by 2030** (aligned to national policy).
- Models **Shark 6 and Atto 2** already "well received"; more EV/PHEV planned.
- **Gharo CKD plant commercial ops in 2HCY26**, 25,000 units/yr scalable to ~50,000.
- Already operating **24 DC fast-charging stations at ~99.9% availability.**
- **Hub Power Holding owns 50% of Mega Motor Company** (the BYD JV vehicle).

## New risks / watch items (specific)
- **CPHGC true-up tariff dispute** is before the appellate tribunal — outcome uncertain; analyst treats it as a prior-year matter already in the price.
- **Receivables (management):** CPHGC **Rs65bn** (the big one), TEL 7.5, TNTPL 8.3, Laraib 5, Narowal 1 — said to be "relatively stable, not risen materially." CPHGC's Rs65bn is the real circular-debt exposure to watch.

## Net effect on the thesis
The analyst/management layer *strengthens* the core read: cheap (5.4–6.5x P/E), de-levering, JV-cash now flowing (Thar plants paying dividends), with **more optionality than the ratios suggest** — EV (selling, plant commissioning), distribution privatisation (3 DISCOs), land repurposing, and E&P/lithium. Principal risks remain rate cycle, the CPHGC tariff dispute, and circular-debt/receivables (CPHGC Rs65bn). No broker target price was stated in these documents.
